# Live voice integration

Status: shared capture, live transcription, Meetings/Dictate UI and guarded in-field dictation implemented in source. Installed call, receiver and headphone acceptance remain separate.

## Ownership

The integration owner on `codex/live-voice-engine` owns the shared capture, recognition, persistence and voice experience. Bounded toolbar and exact-field delivery changes were integrated from separate worker worktrees. One integration owner controls the existing signed Preview installation.

`Sources/LocalVoice/LiveVoiceSession.swift` defines the shared presentation contract. `MeetingModel.voiceSession` and `AppModel.voiceSession` publish `LiveVoiceSnapshot`. This is a projection of the existing owners, not a second state store. Views may display provisional segments differently but must not change or finalize them.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `sessionID` | Stable identity for this capture; changes only for a new session |
| `phase` | `idle`, `preparing`, `listening`, `paused`, `reconnecting`, `finishing`, `completed`, `recoverableFailure` |
| `elapsed` | Session timeline in seconds; device changes do not reset it |
| `sources` | Source, readable name, health, level and optional explanation, independently for microphone and app |
| `segments` | Timed source-labelled words, stable IDs, with `isFinal` distinguishing confirmed words from a revisable tail |
| `recognitionDelayed` | Audio capture can continue while recognition catches up |
| `liveIncomplete` | The live pass missed or doubled words at a boundary or a backlog; live words continue and the saved transcript comes from the original audio |
| `liveStopped` | The live pass stopped for good; the caption says so and the saved transcript comes from the original audio |
| `message` | An actionable session-level explanation when needed |

Meetings keeps its existing `start()`, `stop()`, `cancel()` (keep for later), recovery and recording-review owners. `pause()` and `resume()` affect the same session. `startOffered(_:)` validates the displayed offer before starting its exact source. A stale offer cannot select or start a different app. Dictate retains its existing shortcuts and delivery choice. Live words appear in its workspace and, for eligible external text fields with automatic paste selected, in the captured field. The floating pill stays compact and carries no transcript bubble.

The session remains open across a device change. Unavailable audio is represented on its real timeline and reported; audio is never compressed across a missing interval. Pause creates no speech. Stop flushes the remaining speech before saving. Capture and confirmed words remain recoverable if recognition fails.

One-click offers are available only for sources the metadata detector can identify. Browser capture can include its other tabs. Safari is offered when macOS attributes the WebKit helper to Safari; this is not yet observed on a real Safari call. Another app's WebKit audio is never offered or recorded as Safari. A read that attributes no WebKit helper to Safari lists it as "Safari (its call audio isn't available to Workbench right now)", and admission refuses Start with the reason ([the decision](desktop.md#decisions)). FaceTime and Phone recordings also tap the Mac calling service, where their calls play (#377). The app name is not a meeting title or participant identity. Microphone-only recording remains an explicit choice. Detection stays off until enabled and never starts recording itself.

## Recognition and recovery

Parakeet v2 remains the model. The adapter requests a provisional update after roughly two seconds of new audio and commits eight-second ranges with two seconds of context on each side. These are scheduling parameters, not a measured latency promise. Each request uses fresh decoder state. It retains individual word times and reconciles the last committed word across windows, allowing timing jitter. A boundary the next decode cannot match, or a backlog of more than 60 s of audio, does not stop live words: the words continue by the plain midpoint rule (a backlog skips to the newest audio), the pass is marked incomplete with one note, the live caption says so, and the saved transcript comes from the original audio at finish. The view and the saved live transcript group timed words into source turns (`LiveVoiceTurns`, #377). Each source's words are cut into utterances as the saved-audio path cuts a track (0.6 s of silence, at most 25 s), and the microphone's are also cut where the other side starts or stops speaking. The microphone's copy of the other side, heard on laptop speakers, is dropped by the rule the saved-audio conversation also uses (`SpeakerEcho`): a microphone utterance that mostly overlaps an app utterance in time and repeats its words, or a shorter one whose words the app said in the same order at about the same time. A speaker's utterances stay one turn until the other source speaks or they pause for two seconds; overlapping speakers alternate by utterance, never word by word, and the saved original wording is the conversation's words without labels. Independent identical replies are preserved, and a speaker's provisional words continue their confirmed turn. The service publishes a snapshot when words, the phase, a source's health or a note change, and the meter and clock at most once a second; the transcript view rewrites only from the first turn that changed. Short endings are padded to the model's minimum input rather than discarded. Original capture runs independently of inference.

The selected model is reserved until capture and its last live request finish. The loopback server remains a file-transcription option and does not silently switch to Parakeet. A live processing backlog, missing word timings, callback overflow or failed checkpoint write leaves completion false and retains the original audio. Meetings checks journal identity and each source's saved duration before skipping batch recognition. An incomplete live journal is supporting recovery data, never a complete History result.

Pause and route gaps retain real elapsed time. The source writer records structured gap intervals and pads the corresponding original track with silence; it does not compress later speech across a missing interval. A device change rebinds only the source it affects, and the other keeps recording without a gap (`MeetingRouteRecovery`): a changed Mac output or the chosen app's process going restarts the app tap; a changed microphone device restarts the microphone; a source that stops supplying samples restarts alone. While one route is reconnecting the other is still checked every half second, so a change found there, like a failure its own callback reports, joins the running recovery and shares its cap and retry cadence rather than waiting for it (`MeetingRouteRecovery.polled` says which routes are asked). Each route carries its own callback token, so rebuilding one never discards the other's buffers, and only the restarted track carries the interruption mark. An unavailable source has a bounded recovery period (fifteen seconds, retried every two) before capture stops with retained audio and an explanation; each tick of that recovery is one pure decision (`MeetingRouteRecovery.step`: complete, retry only the routes still silent, give up, or wait), and recovery is complete when the restarted sources deliver again, whatever the other is doing. Pause, resume and finish stop both sources on purpose. A meeting also saves the words heard with the interruption noted. This cannot recover speech that never reached the Mac or arrived while a device was unavailable. Hardware acceptance of the per-source restart is still owed: headphones in and out, AirPods, display audio and the chosen app quitting have not been run on a device since the change; the synthetic checks hold the plan, not the devices.

## Acceptance

Finalized live meetings use meeting format 2, holding the confirmed text without creating another whole-session mixed audio copy. The first such save keeps the exact previous format-1 manifest as `meeting-v1.json`; original tracks remain unchanged. Existing format-1 meetings are still readable and are not bulk migrated. Older binaries cannot open format-2 recordings. New timing sidecars also carry optional gap records, which can exceed older binaries' 1 KiB read limit after repeated interruptions. A binary rollback must retain these folders and use a compatible build to review them; it is not a data rollback.

Join a supported call, accept Start, see progressive words, switch Mac/headphones in both directions, pause/resume, stop, and verify one transcript and original recording in History. Check recognition failure, device disappearance, short replies, simultaneous sources, cancellation, relaunch recovery and a long session. Dictate must show progressive text while preserving its draft and shortcut. Supported fields receive owned-span updates; final cleanup replaces that same span after History commits. Unsupported fields keep one final delivery. Once a live write is attempted, uncertainty must never trigger a duplicate paste. Synthetic checks and renders do not establish physical-device or installed acceptance.

Individual voice identification, automatic recording, calendar-title lookup and automatic sending/scheduling remain outside this change.

`LocalVoice --check-meeting-capture-native NEW_FOLDER [--self-end]` runs the real pipeline without a person: a scratch player app with its own bundle identifier speaks a synthetic two-voice fixture, the production process source lists it, the production tap captures it, the cached Parakeet model recognises it live, and `MeetingModel` finishes and commits it; the receipt holds first sound, first words, the manifest, the live journal and the word error rate. Run it from the signed app (`open -n -W -a "Workbench Preview.app" --args …`) so that app's System Audio Recording permission applies: a refused tap delivers silence, not an error, and the check reports ten seconds of silence as the permission result. Its scratch player is not a catalogued app, so the silent-app line on the page cannot show during this check (the activity read says unknown); only the saved note can. It proves the pipeline on this Mac, not any call app's remote stream; `remoteAudioVerified` stays false until a real call is recorded. See [the 15 October record](verification/2026-10-15-meeting-capture-native/README.md).

## Voice experience

Meetings offers one explicit Start for the displayed call source, with Change source when needed. Start on the proactive offer stays with the call once recording begins (the pill's red dot and Finish are on screen); Workbench's window opens only when that start needs attention. Opening Meetings while recording shows that same session, its source health, elapsed time and live words. A refused System Audio Recording tap delivers silence rather than an error, so when the chosen app has used audio for fifteen seconds and no sound has arrived, the app's source line says so and says where the switch is in Privacy & Security, in the words Home's Permissions row uses for this macOS (`MeetingSilenceHint`, from elapsed time, heard sound and the app's audio activity, with the place from `MacPermission.callAudio.settingsPath`; never for microphone-only, an inactive app, an app the catalogue does not know or unreadable metadata, the last two both reading as unknown activity); the line clears once sound arrives and the source's health stays what capture reports. The saved notes say the same, from the same seam, when the app's track stayed silent throughout. It is a hedged line, never a record: a broken tap is silent too. Pause/Resume keeps the session; Finish saves it. Provisional words are visually quieter. The selectable native transcript preserves selection and scroll position while the reader looks back, with Latest words to return to the end. Dictate uses the same transcript component without speaker labels. The compact toolbar exposes the same Pause/Resume/Finish actions through its existing chooser and keeps its existing geometry and focus behavior. A recording or finishing meeting keeps those controls whatever Hide toolbar says, and Quit while a meeting records asks first: Finish meeting saves the transcript, then quits; Keep recording cancels the quit; Quit and keep for later keeps the audio to transcribe next time.

After a successful meeting save, its transcript stays visible. Prepare follow-up opens the existing Hand off review with that exact transcript, independent of any other History selection. The prepared request asks for purpose, decisions, evidenced commitments, missing owners/dates and the most useful draft; social calls may need no action. Interpreting context uses the chosen handoff destination, not a new automatic model run. Nothing is sent or scheduled.

### Guarded automatic finish

Finish when call audio ends defaults on for supported app recordings and can be changed in Recording options. It first requires observed activity from the chosen app, its recognized helpers or, for FaceTime and Phone, the Mac calling service. Both input and output must then remain inactive for 20 seconds, followed by a 10-second cancellable countdown. A source using audio, even silent or muted audio, remains active. Unknown metadata, Pause and reconnecting audio revoke the countdown and require fresh active evidence. Keep recording disables automatic Finish for that session and is reachable in the page, the meeting's row on the menu-bar panel and in Home's Current work, and the toolbar chooser. Microphone-only and unclassified sources remain manual. This is an audio-activity heuristic, not a meeting-provider call-end API; a provider that leaves audio running still needs Finish. A recording Workbench finishes itself, when call audio ends, at the two-hour limit or when a source cannot reconnect, keeps a first note saying so and why (`MeetingSelfFinish`), so its transcript in History says why its words end there; Home's tile still shows a note about what the recording lost before it. Keep recording acts only during the countdown and on the recording it was shown for, so a late click never switches automatic finish off.

### The two-hour limit

From five minutes before two hours, the page's caption, the meeting's row on the panel and in Home and the toolbar chooser say when Workbench will finish the recording (“Finishes at 2 hours in 4 min”, whole minutes left, then “under a minute”; `MeetingTimeLimit`); at the limit it finishes like Finish meeting, with its note. Paused and reconnecting time count, as they do on the clock the page shows, and while paused the caption says “Up to 2 hours, pauses included”. Decided 20 October 2026 (#377): a recording keeps one timeline and a pause is written into both tracks as silence once audio returns, so paused time costs the disk and the recognition the limit protects; the writer, the segment plan and recovery all bound that same timeline. Revisit if people pause long meetings and lose their end to the limit: that would need the writer to stop filling pauses and the plan and recovery to accept a longer timeline, a saved-recording change.

### Live words in another app

The native path requires a captured nonsecure readable field, writable SelectedTextRange and SelectedText attributes, and focus/value/selection notifications. It replaces only the owned speech span, never the whole document or clipboard-pastes every chunk. Full field value and caret are checked before and after each write. User input, app/focus/selection changes, errors or uncertainty permanently close that capture's writer. IPC and update cadence are bounded. Final cleanup updates the same span only after History is durable; no fallback paste follows an established live owner failing. A stopped writer instead leaves the complete transcript, as dictated, on the clipboard; Restore clipboard applies only after a confirmed live insertion. When nothing was ever written to the field the result is an ordinary copy with its Paste with ⌘V cue, and the original audio is released like any delivered capture. Cancel restores the original selected words only when exact ownership can still be verified. Otherwise the field is left alone, the original audio is retained and the result explains that review is needed. Explicit clipboard delivery remains clipboard-only.

Dictated words fit the insertion point (#14). Both deliveries, the live span and the final paste, pass the dictated text through one pure rule set in `InsertionBoundary.swift` with the field's text either side of the caret or selection, read through the same Accessibility reads that already verify the field: the live owner's initial read and the final paste's own pre-paste snapshot, so the fit adds no read. The rules: a space before when the previous character is a letter, digit, emoji or closing punctuation (`. , ; : ! ? ) ] } %`, a closing quote, or a straight quote an earlier one opened) and the dictated text does not start with closing punctuation or a line break; none after whitespace, a line break, `( [ {` or an opening quote; a space after when the next character is a letter, digit, emoji, `( [ {` or an opening quote; never a double space. A sentence starts at the start of the field, after a line break (a line holding only list or quote markers such as `- * + • > # [ ] 1. 1)` still counts, and a bare marker gains its space), or after `. ! ?` (closing quotes and brackets may follow) and whitespace; a full stop ending a title or Latin abbreviation (`Dr. Mr. Mrs. Ms. Prof. St. Mt. vs. e.g. i.e. cf. approx.`) does not end a sentence. At a sentence start an all-lowercase first word is capitalised, and `iPhone`, `github.com`, `e.g.` and code are left alone. Mid-sentence, Workbench lowercases only everyday words a recogniser capitalises at the start of an utterance (the, a, it, we, this, and so on); names and everything else keep what you said. The list is closed and built in (articles, pronouns and their contractions, conjunctions, prepositions, determiners, auxiliaries and modals, common adverbs and discourse words); `I` and its contractions stay, a dictionary spelling keeps its capital even when it is in the list (a dictionary entry `Will`), so does a word already written with that capital mid-sentence in the field or later in the dictation, and a title abbreviation such as `Dr.`, all-caps acronyms, numbers, URLs, code, `McDonald` and `O'Brien` keep the dictated spelling. One final full stop is dropped when the sentence continues after it or the field already ends it there; before a capital or a line break, or after an abbreviation (`Dr.`, `etc.`), it stays. Replacing a selection measures the text around the selection. The live span's first partial establishes the prefix and every later partial and the final cleanup keep it; a repeated partial that fits to the inserted text neither reads nor writes the field. Secure fields read as no value, so their words go in as dictated; an unreadable value or an unsupported range changes nothing. The clipboard rules are unchanged: the clipboard holds the transcript as dictated, the fitted words are copied only once the paste is about to be posted, every earlier return (changed focus, paste unavailable, a changed clipboard, cancellation) leaves the transcript copied, and once the paste has been processed the transcript is copied back when Workbench still owns the clipboard, as the live span already leaves it; Restore clipboard restores the previous contents as before. History saves the transcript as dictated and nothing presses Return. `--check-insertion-boundary` runs the 107 synthetic fixtures; `--check-core` includes them.

[Claude's native quick entry](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12626668-use-quick-entry-with-claude-desktop-on-mac) establishes the real-time composer baseline. [Apple's selected-text attribute](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applicationservices/kaxselectedtextattribute) and [attribute-setting API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applicationservices/1460434-axuielementsetattributevalue) support the cross-app approach, but do not guarantee a receiver implements it correctly. External app compatibility must be established in installed testing; an owned NSTextView fixture is not that evidence.

UI integration checks include the automatic-finish policy, known helper processes, metadata failures, exact-field span ownership, uncertain-write fallback, Cancel rollback and durable original recovery. Native transcript renders use synthetic words in light/dark listening, paused, reconnecting, finishing and completed states, and a silent-app state carrying its hint.
